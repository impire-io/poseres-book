## LinkedIn

I own a robot lawnmower. Every time it sets out across the lawn it finds the same tree trunk, wedges itself against it, spins its wheels, and waits for me to come rescue it. It has been stuck on that trunk more times than I can count, and it will get stuck on it again tomorrow. The brain inside was finished before the mower ever saw my garden. Nothing it experiences changes it.

Almost every AI system you use today works this way. Trained once, frozen, shipped. I think that's the wrong foundation for machines that live in the world, and I've been building the alternative: a brain that starts small and never stops learning.

I wrote a book about it. Sixteen chapters on why frozen models fail, what a sensorimotor triplet is, and how a population of competing frames learns structure nobody specified. The main text asks for no background at all; the engineering detail sits in clearly marked boxes you can skip or hunt for.

It's free to read: https://impire.io/poseres-book/
