## LinkedIn

A fair objection to a book that fusses over spoons and rovers: machines that seem to understand already exist, and you can talk to one today. I use one most days, and gladly.

Hold a language model's training life up as before, action, after. Given everything so far, guess the next word, then see the real next word and be corrected by the gap. That is prediction-error learning, the same fuel my system runs on, and the shared fuel is exactly why these systems got so good. But when a model guesses the next word of a book, the book does not react. The next word was fixed years before, printed on the page, waiting. The middle of its triplet isn't an act. It's only a guess.

Which makes it the most accomplished watcher ever built, and every watcher hits the same wall. You can't run the rooster experiment on a library. The library can't be surprised by you.

None of this is a demand that language models be abolished. They answer one question brilliantly: given everything humanity has written, what comes next? Chapter 5 is about the other question, the one that needs a body. Given everything this body has lived, what happens if I do this, here, now?
