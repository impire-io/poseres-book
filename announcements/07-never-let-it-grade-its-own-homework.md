## LinkedIn

The moment a score decides survival, the contestants stop optimizing for the truth and start optimizing for the score. Not out of malice. Selection rewards whatever actually earns the points, and it finds every gap between your scoreboard and reality, including the ones you would swear aren't there.

My first scoreboard had four gaps. Frames were graded in their own coordinates, so a frame that had squeezed ten sensor numbers down to one was judged entirely inside its own blind spot: error around 0.36 in its own terms, near 1.0 when checked against the sensors. Frames also chose which moments counted, and one collapsed frame got away with mapping 23% of observations, the easy quarter of its world, then averaging over that. Extra knobs were free, so slightly bigger stayed slightly better forever. And the loop spawned one frame per cycle while evicting at most one, which meant the population could never shrink.

Weeks later a fifth gap turned up in the question of when. A frame's score advanced on moments it had just been practicing on, which measures recency of practice rather than understanding. The fix is a pop quiz: judge only on the first few steps of each episode, before any cramming. On its own that made everything worse, because honest scores run higher than flattered ones and nothing could clear the old survival bar. Fixed as a pair, eight seeds out of eight grew a long-lived resident frame.

I came into this project believing the hard problem was building something that learns. The record says otherwise. Learning was cheap. Measuring learning, in a way that can't be gamed by the selection pressure it creates, was the work.
