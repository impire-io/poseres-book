## LinkedIn

The last chapter ended with praise working as a label rather than fuel, so I built the label: an applauded completion counts fuller than a plain one, and the approval channel is read nowhere else. Then I gave the parent something to want that the pupil did not. It applauds cobblestone, never sticks, and the teaching tape grew twelve lessons walking to the stone wall with applause at the end of each one.

The pupils expected that applause at ceiling, measured the same day. Cobblestone mined across 24 of them: zero. Not one pupil, not one stone. The label pays one step away from a finished dig, and nothing in the composition valued the walk, which is where all the cost is. I had predicted 14 to 20 pupils would reach the stone. First time in the arc I over-predicted rather than under-predicted.

The two roads out were both expensive. Hand the brain a ladder of subgoals, which means declaring the order myself. Or build learned planning, a research program measured in months. The third road only shows up once you notice what a demonstration is: forty-five walks through the wood chain and twelve to the wall, every one recorded in the same sensor readings the bot thinks with, in order, with the applause in the right place. The order was in the teaching all along. Nothing in the brain was keeping it.

So it keeps one recipe per finished item now, and follows it with a pointer that advances when the bot gets within a block. Same cohort, same demonstrations, against the label's floor of zero: 24 of 24 reached the stone, 3,129 cobblestone gain events. Reach was never missing from the mechanism.
