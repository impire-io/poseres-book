## LinkedIn

Forty-five times I drove the bot's hands through the whole chain: dig the log, craft planks, craft sticks. The knowledge stuck. Every taught brain carries the chain, and whenever anything persuades one to start, the whole chain runs. Then I gave 24 of them a perfect sense of direction, and their median time at the workshop came out at 99.98%. One hundred twenty thousand steps of standing exactly where the log was. Not one log dug.

The missing piece got a name that day: election, choosing on purpose to begin a sequence you already know. So I added one term to the action choice, valuing how much dig progress each candidate action would add. Same graduates, same hold. Twenty-four of 24 gained a log, 286 across the arm, against zero logs in 24 runs without it.

Then I took the crutch out. That first version read progress by peeking at the game's own bookkeeping. Swapped for the brain's own prediction, election fell to 11 of 24, because the brain's model of its progress was blurrier than the quantity it had to rank: a median error of 0.0612 where a single dig tick is worth 0.083. So I built a second small predictor beside the frames, one model per action, sharp about the moments a number jumps. Its error came out at 0.0081, and election went back to 24 of 24, with 303 logs and 13 of 24 running the full chain. That is more than double what the peeking version managed. The student beat its oracle.
