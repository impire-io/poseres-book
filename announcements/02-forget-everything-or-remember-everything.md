## LinkedIn

Ask for a brain that never stops learning and you get pulled toward one of two cliffs. Lean one way and it keeps overwriting itself until the old skills are gone. Lean the other way and it keeps adding to itself until it is an unusable hoard.

June 2026, my own system went off the second cliff while I watched. Prototype v3 had passed its most important test: does the brain grow itself to match the size of the world it is in? I had one rule standing between me and believing that, a gate I had imposed on myself, which was to run it longer and read the spread rather than the average. At 30 cycles instead of 18, the pass dropped from six of eight runs to three of eight.

The population chart was the part that made me laugh. One new model per cycle, a clean straight line, no plateau. The eviction rule was there in the code, and it ran. I had written the survival bar so that the more crowded the population got, the easier it became to survive.

Worse than the bloat was the cover-up. Each small model measured how predictable the world was in its own coordinates, so the models that had collapsed into a nearly empty view posted the best scores.
