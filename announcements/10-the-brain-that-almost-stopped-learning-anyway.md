## LinkedIn

Part 1 of this book promised a brain that never stops learning. Parts 2 and 3 built one. Then I measured whether the promise was being kept, and found that it wasn't.

I was scanning the error-against-size surface at several training budgets, the longest sixteen times the usual length. Frames of middle sizes got worse with the extra experience. Not noisier. Worse, roughly doubling their honest prediction error by the time the budget had quadrupled, on every seed. Same frame, same world, same learning rule. The only added ingredient was more life.

The mechanism is weight growth. Each step still reduces today's error on today's data, but the weights inflate doing it, and a network with bloated weights turns rigid. It doesn't forget what it knew. It loses the ability to be gently changed. Then the selection tournament made it structural: in a population where mid-sized frames sicken with age and small ones don't, the long game belongs to the small and rot-proof regardless of how well they model the world.

The repair touches magnitude and nothing else. Each weight tensor gets a ceiling set modestly above its size at birth, direction untouched, learning never paused. Rot eliminated, and the scaled landing at true-size 20 rose from median 6 to median 10. Continuity isn't a property you design in once. Its failure modes don't announce themselves. They quietly select for smaller minds.
