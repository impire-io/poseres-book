## LinkedIn

After ten thousand episodes of life, what does this brain contain? For most continual-learning systems the honest inventory includes an archive: the model, plus a curated scrapbook of lived moments to re-study forever, plus a policy for what to paste in and what to tear out. Lose the scrapbook and the next lesson starts destroying old competence again.

PRA's inventory is the population of frames: their sizes, their weights, their running scores. That is the whole estate. Outside a small fixed window the drive layer keeps for its bookkeeping, no observation is retained. Its memory footprint on day one thousand is the same as on day one. Ask it about a specific afternoon last month and there is nothing there to answer with.

That buys something and costs something, and the chapter prices both. No growing storage, no growing rehearsal cost, no curator deciding in advance what future-you will need. Against that: a one-time event whose lesson stops paying rent will eventually be competed away. This is a brain, not a log. If your application needs an audit trail, keep one.

What the system does have is a pause button. The complete learned state writes to a file, and a run resumed from that file is byte-for-byte identical to the run that never stopped. The chapter ends with the smallest crack that standard ever showed: one telemetry number off by one ULP, traced to a snapshot writing the frame groups sorted by size while the live engine held them in birth order. Sorting is a mutation.
