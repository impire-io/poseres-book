## LinkedIn

In July I had a judge pulse a well-done onto a sensor channel at the exact tick a stick-craft landed, forty-five times per pupil. The brains never reliably came to expect it. Fourteen of 24 ended with a specific expectation against a bar of 18, and five ended anti-predictive at the very tick the praise always arrived.

Same gate, same judge, same channel, same 45 approvals, rerun with the event pathway now shipped inside the brain: 24 of 24, and not narrowly. Every pupil ended expecting the well-done at 1.000 on the completion tick and 0.000 off it, already half-formed inside the first five approvals. The frames' own predictions ran alongside as a context row and reproduced July's exact digits, which is about as close as an experiment comes to proving it was fair both times.

Then the tempting step. The brain can expect the praise perfectly, so make it want the praise: one term valuing the predicted change in the approval channel. At the smallest dose tried, approvals earned fell from 1,888 to 572 and sticks from 2,473 to 863. Higher doses collapsed into log-hoarding with zero sticks. Praise here is a pulse, 1.0 on the tick and 0.0 after, so every familiar continuation of the praised loop comes to predict approval falling, and the term taxes it for that. I had pre-registered a watch for sycophancy. The measured failure mode was avoidance.

My pupil learns most from praise when the praise is a label on the moment, not fuel for reaching it. https://impire.io/poseres-book/part-6-teachers/15-a-label-not-fuel.html
