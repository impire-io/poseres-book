## LinkedIn

How much brain does a world need? Here is a way to make that concrete. Play this game with anything that moves: the smallest number of knobs you would need on a control panel to describe it completely. A playground swing takes one, its angle. A boat on a lake takes three. One hand lands somewhere over twenty before you have finished counting it.

A camera watching that swing delivers a million pixels, sixty times a second, for a world with one knob in it. Finding the few knobs behind the many numbers is most of what understanding a world is. Today the number gets picked by a person in the lab, before training starts. Chapter 1's brains had their knowledge frozen at the factory; this freezes the shape of what can be known.

By early summer 2026 the v4 prototype was answering the question on its own in a toy world of three hidden knobs, on eight random runs, almost every time. So I built the real engine, about forty times faster, which made bigger worlds affordable: 20 knobs, 35, 50, with observations three times wider. At every one of those sizes the answer came back one.

It did not crash, and it was not the old cheating. It failed politely, returning a clean, wrong, tiny answer, and it shaped the next several weeks. Chapter 3: https://impire.io/poseres-book/part-1-the-problem/03-the-question-nobody-answers.html
