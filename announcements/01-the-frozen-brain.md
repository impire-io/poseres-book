## LinkedIn

Nobody would accept a frozen brain in a person. It would mean hiring someone whose last day of learning was their final exam. We accept it for almost every machine: the studying stops, whatever the brain knows at that moment gets copied into the product, and nothing it experiences after that changes it.

Freezing mostly works, which is exactly why it is everywhere. A frozen brain is predictable, cheap to stamp out a million times, and when it makes a mistake it makes the same mistake every time, so an engineer can hunt that mistake down. There is a reason nobody wants their bank's software to improvise.

The trouble starts when anything moves, and three things move all the time: the world, the task, and the body. The body is the sneaky one. My robot mower's blades are a little duller every month, so the same motor command cuts a little less than it used to. The brain steering it is exactly as worn as the day it left the factory, which is to say not at all, and it can't even notice.
