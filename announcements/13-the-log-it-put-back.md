## LinkedIn

In July I gave the brain Minecraft. One bot, one vanilla server on a small Linux box, no script, no goals, no rewards. The rules were on paper before boot: any sustained gathering of materials counts as above chance, and a single craft is the headline. Eight pilot bots acting with no learning at all never finished digging a single block, so that was the floor.

For four days in late July it gathered. 449 completed digs, 605 pieces of dirt, 448 handfuls of leaf litter, 15 oak saplings, 8 wheat seeds. Everything it picked up it later placed back into the world somewhere else. And once, on July 26, it dug out an oak log, the single hardest acquisition in the run. The recipe that turns a log into planks needs one move: put the log in the grid. It held the log for about four minutes, then placed it back into the ground and never picked up another.

Then the gathering stopped, and the crafting grid stayed busy anyway. The bot pressed "take the offer" 222,305 times. Across all 5,669,662 recorded steps of the run, the count of steps on which an offer was showing is zero.

A clean null on the headline question, and the texture of the null is the finding. The brain made contact with every part of the machinery, relentlessly, for two and a half weeks, and never produced a sequence: get the right thing, stage the right thing, take the result. The gap this run measured is chaining. Chapter 13 is the full run report, including the three hours when my disk filled up and 57,219 steps of the record aged out.
